# Getting started and help

## Install and update

1. Download **ExoLauncher-Setup.exe** from the [latest release](https://github.com/UhhErix/ExoLauncher/releases/latest).
2. Run it, choose Install, then Launch Exo. First run asks for your game stores, your music service, Discord and your capture keys, offers to remove the apps Exo replaces (PC > Apps lists them first later), and ends with **Best settings for this PC** (Exo's power plan, driver profile, network and drive settings, HDR for HDR games and RTX Video HDR, whichever your PC has) and Lucid: **Use best settings** sets them, **Not now** leaves them for the PC room. The installer fetches what Exo needs beside itself, the dictation model among it, so everything is ready on first run.
3. Exo finds your games. It checks for a new version each time it starts; when there is one, it asks before you start, with **Update now**, and never while Exo is already open. **Settings > General** shows it too, with **Install update**. Your settings, library and sign-ins stay.

Exo needs **Windows 11 x64** and installs for your Windows user only. The installer isn't code-signed yet, so SmartScreen may warn the first time: choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**. Each release lists the installer's SHA-256.

To uninstall, open **Windows Settings > Apps > Installed apps**, choose **Exo Launcher**, then **Uninstall**. Your games, settings and accounts stay on your PC.

Exo's icon sits in the taskbar's hidden icons while it runs: press it to open Exo, or right-press it for **Open** and **Quit**.

## Find anything

Press **Ctrl+K**, or the Exo mark at the top left, to reach any room, game, action, music control, or Discord's DMs and servers. Ctrl+K brings back your last search, selected, so typing replaces it.

## Look and feel

**Settings > General**:
- **Text size** and **Reduce motion**. **Settings > Library** has **Show tools and soundtracks** (servers, SDKs and soundtracks in the Library).

Every screen gets the same layout: on a 1440p or 4K screen Exo draws the 1080p layout larger, so it looks the same, only sharper.

## Library

- **A game is missing.** Press **More** on the grid's title line, then **Refresh library**. Exo reads every store again. For Epic, GOG or Amazon, sign in under **Settings > Library**. For a game outside any store, press **More > Add a game** and pick its .exe, or **Find a game in a folder**.
- **Play, install and update.** Click a game to open its page, or right-click it for Play or Install and Open folder. **Continue** at the top keeps the games you're playing one click from Play. Downloads and updates show on the game's card. Downloads can pause while you play and wait for the hours you set in **Settings > Downloads**.
- **Folders.** **More > New folder**: name it, pick a color and tick its games. A game can be in as many folders as you like: right-click it and tick or untick its folders, or pick **New folder** to start one with it. Right-click a folder to edit or delete it (its games go back to the grid). An open folder's sort menu orders its games by **Folder order** (the order you added them) or as the Library sorts. A folder is also how you keep favorites.
- **A folder's picture.** In **Edit folder**, press **Add picture** (or drop a PNG, JPEG or WebP under 15 MB on the sheet), then drag and zoom it in the cropper and press **Done**. **Remove picture** puts the games' covers back.
- **Hide a game.** Right-click it and choose **Hide**: it leaves the grid, **Continue** and its folders. The sort menu's **Show > Hidden games** lists the games you hid, each with **Unhide**; Ctrl+K still finds them.
- **Uninstall.** **Uninstall** in a game's **Properties** removes it through its store, after Exo asks you once. When the store asks you to confirm, or only its app can remove the game (Xbox), that window shows. A game you added yourself says **Remove**, and Exo leaves its files where they are.
- **Properties.** The sliders button on an installed game's page, or right-click its card: launch options, language, betas, checking its files, **Rebuild shaders** (Call of Duty: when colors or textures look wrong after a game or driver update, the game builds its shaders again as it next starts), moving it to another drive, its DLC or optional content (for example a high-resolution texture pack) on or off, and **Artwork** to replace its cover with your own picture. Anything that downloads or deletes shows its size and asks first. Cloud saves, updates, the Steam overlay and Steam Input have no switch: Exo keeps them right for each Steam game (cloud saves on, updates kept, the overlay only for games that sell through Steam, play online with friends or Remote Play Together, or use Steam Input). Where Steam Input maps your controller, **Controller in Steam** opens Steam's own page for its layout.
- **Optimize.** On Modern Warfare 4's page, press **Optimize** and Exo sets the game's own settings for your PC and setup. The graphics follow [BenchmarKing's guide](https://youtu.be/tjFDuyc8hSY), which measures every setting: his competitive list, with the costly settings low and what costs almost nothing kept up. On-demand Texture Streaming and Deferred Physics Quality aren't in a file Exo may write, so they stay yours. The rest is where the guides, benchmarks and shared configs agree: borderless on your screen's highest refresh (the mouse kept in the game with a second screen), your card's low-latency mode with V-Sync and frame generation off, render threads for your processor, full render resolution with your card's own upscaler and sharpening at the preset your screen suits (DLSS's faster CNN model on an RTX 20 or 30), no blur of any kind, textures and the video memory budget for your card's memory, the widest field of view for your screen with aiming scaled to it, raw mouse input (no acceleration, filtering or smoothing, ADS multipliers eased in, no scroll delay), music and cutscenes muted and reduce tinnitus on so footsteps carry, MW4's headphones mix in stereo without Windows Sonic when you listen on headphones, no quality drop when you hold still, and no intro videos. The line then says **Applied**. Close the game first; Exo never changes them while it runs. Your resolution, voice and master volume, sensitivity and keys stay yours. Call of Duty no longer asks to start in safe mode, which would reset these settings: Exo's Stop lets it quit cleanly, and after a crash Exo clears the mark it leaves. On Rocket League's page (Epic or Steam), **Optimize** keeps the game's own frame pacing as Psyonix ships it (undoing the "tweaks" that switch its frame cap off or cost frames for no less lag), keeps full resolution, sets 16x texture filtering on a card with its own memory, turns off motion blur, depth of field, bloom and ambient occlusion, and keeps the ball's ground marker, its shadow on walls and see-through goalposts. Resolution, display mode, V-Sync, the frame rate list, detail levels, anti-aliasing and weather are saved with your Rocket League account, so set them in its own Video menu. On Phantom Blade Zero's page, **Optimize** sets borderless full screen, which runs at your screen's highest refresh and never switches modes on Alt+Tab. The game keeps its whole Video page (upscaler, Output Scale, frame generation, Reflex, V-Sync, the frame cap, quality) in a sealed file it checks as it loads, so that page stays yours. On CONTROL Resonant's page, **Optimize** follows [his guide](https://youtu.be/peF6jgKQCeQ) for your card and your screen, with your card's own upscaler and frame generation: textures for your card's memory (Low or Medium on 8 GB, Medium on 12 GB), volumetrics and reflections below Ultra, and ray tracing off under 12 GB. On Hell is Us's page, **Optimize** sets [his settings](https://youtu.be/BFdz1s7gCRk) for your card in the game's own file: Medium textures on 8 GB, DLSS Quality with Reflex on an RTX card and XeSS on any other, and with Exo's frame generation in the game, the game's DLSS Frame Generation on at 2x. Keep that on in its menu, since Exo's frame generation runs through it. The first time, press **Optimize** and play once: the line says **Applies after you play once**, and Exo writes it when you quit the game. Leave the game's graphics preset alone afterwards: he found picking one changes the resolution DLSS runs at until you switch DLSS's mode. Red Dead Redemption 2, Cyberpunk 2077, Alan Wake 2, Dying Light: The Beast, Dying Light 2, Crimson Desert, Star Wars Outlaws, Oblivion Remastered, Metal Gear Solid Delta and Remnant II have Optimize too, with his settings for your card wherever a setting has a line in the game's own file; the rest of the menu stays yours. Counter-Strike 2 and Path of Exile 2 have the window mode only (and DLSS in Path of Exile 2), since nobody has measured their settings. **Applied** means Exo found and wrote everything it asked for. **Partly optimized** means the game's file doesn't hold all of it, and **Settings not found** that it holds none, usually after a game update. Optimize never caps your frame rate. On the line for a game whose file holds its window mode, choose **Borderless** or **Fullscreen**: picking one applies Optimize with that mode, and Exo holds it, setting the mode back whenever the game is closed and it has changed (as the game closes, as Exo starts and just before you press Play here), until you put it back. Borderless is the default. In true Fullscreen Exo's FPS counter and banners can't show over the game, and Alt+Tab is slower. Rocket League has no choice, since its display mode is saved with your account. Once pressed, Optimize stays on: when a game update resets the game's settings, Exo sets them again as the game closes, says so, and the line reads **Applied again after an update** until you next play. A setting you changed in the game's own menu stays yours, apart from that held window mode, and pressing **Applied** puts everything back and stops it.
- **Steam Family.** Games your Steam Family shares show in the Library next to your own and install through Steam. One that plays offline (single-player or on one screen, with no anti-cheat) starts with Steam in its own offline mode, and Steam goes back online once you stop playing; one that plays online, or has an update waiting, starts with Steam online, and Play says why. Offline mode needs Steam to remember your sign-in.
- **Steam offline mode.** **Settings > Library > Game stores > Offline mode**, under Steam, restarts Steam out of sight in its own offline mode until you turn it off. Steam doesn't switch while a Steam game runs or while it downloads, and says why.
- **A store's servers are down.** When Steam can't reach its servers (or hangs on Loading User Data), Exo says so once. Press **Play offline** there, or Play on a Steam game, and Exo restarts Steam in its own offline mode after asking; it needs Steam to remember your sign-in. Exo switches Steam back online once its servers answer, and says so. GOG games start without GOG Galaxy anyway, and the Epic Games Launcher goes offline by itself. The EA app and Ubisoft Connect have **Go offline** in their own menus; Battle.net plays offline only the few games that support it, and Riot games need Riot's servers.
- **More than one account.** **Settings > Library** lists the Steam accounts Steam remembers and the Riot accounts Exo saved: **Switch** restarts that store's client as the other account, and **Add account** opens its own sign-in. Epic, GOG and Amazon have their own Account menu.
- **Steam stays current, on its beta.** Steam downloads its own updates while it runs and installs them only as it next starts, which is when "Updating Steam" shows. Exo starts Steam again out of sight once one is waiting, while no game runs and Steam's window is closed, so the update is in before you press Play. Exo also puts Steam on its Beta Update once (Steam's own Client Beta Participation); leave it there and Exo leaves it.
- **Epic's new launcher.** On a PC with the old Epic Games Launcher, **Settings > Library > Epic > Account > Switch to Epic's new launcher** downloads Epic's installer from Epic and opens it; it moves your games over and takes out the old launcher.
- **Quiet stores.** Steam's and Epic's own pop-ups and sounds stay off for every account on this PC, Steam's controller notes before a game ("Grab a controller and kick back!") included, and the Steam overlay is off for every Steam game that doesn't need it. Hours add up every Steam and Epic account on this PC.

## PC

The PC room is this PC, on one page: This PC (what it's made of, with Best settings, Upscalers and Apps on its line), Updates and Shaders. Press your graphics card for the driver and Exo's profile, your screen for Display and HDR, and your motherboard for your BIOS, a restart into it or into Windows' recovery menu, Secure Boot and the TPM.
- **Driver and profile** (Graphics) shows your NVIDIA driver and NVIDIA's newest for your card, Game Ready or Studio, looked for at most once an hour. **Update** installs it clean and driver-only (on a GTX 10 series or older card, which Game Ready drivers no longer support, NVIDIA's newest driver for it), with no NVIDIA app, PhysX or HDMI audio; Exo puts your profile back after. It waits while a game runs or you're in a Discord call. On an AMD Radeon or Intel graphics, the same row shows AMD's or Intel's newest driver for your card: **Update** installs AMD Software: Adrenalin Edition with its driver (Fluid Motion Frames needs AMD Software), or Intel's driver alone, without Intel Graphics Software. Exo never restarts your PC; the row says when the new driver needs a restart. Exo's driver profile (lowest latency, with no frame cap) applies with one press, and **Import** takes a Profile Inspector file. On an AMD Radeon, Exo's profile (Chill, Boost, Super Resolution and Image Sharpening off, FreeSync on, V-Sync left to each game, FSR 4 for FSR 3.1 games, Anti-Lag on) applies the same way, with Undo, and Exo turns Fluid Motion Frames on for each game it suits and off for competitive games and games with frame generation of their own; a game's page says which (it needs AMD Software running). On an Intel Arc or Iris Xe, Exo's profile (Endurance Gaming and sharpening off, Low latency on, Adaptive Sync on, Intel's prebuilt shaders) applies the same way, with Undo, and a game's page can set XeSS frame generation for that game. If the driver doesn't answer, it lists what to set in AMD's or Intel's own app.
- **HDR** (Display) turns HDR on only while a game with real HDR runs; each game's page can set Auto, Always or Never. **SDR looks right in HDR** draws the desktop with gamma 2.2 while HDR is on, so blacks stay black.
- **Exo's network settings** (Best settings) turn off the adapter settings that add delay or drop the link (Energy-Efficient Ethernet, Intel's DMA coalescing, buffers an optimizer shrank) in one Windows prompt; the connection drops for a few seconds, so it waits while you're in a call or a game. On Wi-Fi there's nothing to press: while a game runs, Exo pauses Windows' background scans and keeps the radio at full power on battery.
- **Display** has your screen's refresh rate, color format and depth (only what the screen itself takes), scaling (with **Override the scaling set by games**), color from brightness to hue, and RTX Video; a change applies and stays. A screen off its best shows **Best for this screen** in **Needs you** (with RTX Video HDR on, on a screen running HDR with an RTX card), and **Use** sets it.
- **Best settings**, on This PC's line, has **Use best settings** while something is left to set, and a green check or a red X on each: Exo's power plan for your processor, the driver profile for your card, your network adapter's settings, your drives' write-cache flushing and weekly optimize, HDR for HDR games and RTX Video HDR on a screen running HDR.
- **Needs you** shows only what holds your PC back (RAM below its rated speed, Resizable BAR off, a power plan that slows your processor, GPU scheduling off on a card with frame generation; on a PC with two graphics chips, **Graphics for games** asks Windows to run your games on the fast one). Exo keeps Windows' Game Mode on by itself. **Performance** keeps your chipset driver up to date: AMD's on a Ryzen, Intel's Chipset Device Software on an Intel processor where it's installed. Exo installs a newer one while you're away (once nobody is using the PC: ten minutes without input, or one with it locked, and nothing playing, no call, no other program at work, no game, the PC plugged in), or now with **Update**. It never restarts your PC; the row says when a restart finishes the update. **Settings > Downloads > Keep the AMD chipset driver up to date** (or Intel's) turns it off.

## Upscalers and Shaders

- **Upscalers** (PC; a game's page shows where it stands and opens it) update DLSS, FSR and XeSS files a game already has. Close the game first. **Restore**, in the game's Properties, puts the game's own files back. A game with anti-cheat changes only after you allow it for that game, at your own risk; Riot games are never changed. DLSS presets are part of Exo's driver profile, on an NVIDIA graphics card.
- **Frame generation.** On a GeForce RTX 20 or 30 (3x and 4x on an RTX 40), a game with DLSS Frame Generation has a **Frame generation** line on its page: pick 2x, 3x or 4x and the game's own DLSS Frame Generation switch runs NVIDIA's at that multiplier, through the MFG Unlocker mod. On a Radeon or Intel Arc, a game whose only frame generation is DLSS's gets a switch for OptiScaler instead. It stays off until you turn it on for a game, Exo keeps the mod on its newest release, and turning it off removes every file it added.
- **Shaders** (PC) precompiles games' shaders. **Refresh** finds what to compile and **Compile** works through the queue; press a game to add it or take it out, and drag to change the order. A game's Shaders row opens it here. Nothing runs until you press one of them, and it stops soon after: after a graphics driver update, **Refresh** shows the games to compile again. It skips a game Windows already compiled for your card.
- **Auto tune** (PC; a GeForce or a Radeon) is off until you press it. One press looks for a lower voltage or a faster clock, tests each step, and shows what it measured before and after. It warns you first about your warranty and crashes, goes back to stock by itself if a game starts or anything goes wrong, is never part of Best settings, and **Undo** is one press. Processors are not offered.

## Store

Browse, search and wishlist games. **Get on Steam** and the other buttons open the store's own app (or its page when the app isn't installed) to buy. A game you already own, on any store and installed or not, shows **Library** instead, which opens it in your Library. Prices are in your Windows region's currency, and a game's system requirements are checked against your PC's parts. Wishlisted games on sale show on the Store tab.

## Discord

**Discord** is **Exo's own client**; there is no Discord app to install. Open the room, then on your phone open Discord, go to **You > Scan QR code** and scan the code; confirm on the phone. Exo never sees your password.

- **Home.** The room opens on Home: what is happening right now. Where everyone is: each game, voice room and song with the faces of who is there and one press to go too; who is in voice across your servers, with one press to join; unread DMs and mentions; and who is online. Friends who use Exo see the game you opened from Exo on their Home, and Discord and its apps don't. It is off until you turn on **Share my games with friends on Exo** in Settings › Discord › Privacy; games you hide or add yourself are never named.
- **Messages and Servers.** The band across the top of the room holds Home, Messages and Servers with their counts, then what wants you now, then you. Messages opens your most recent conversation without marking it read; it stays unread until you do something in it. A line of each message shows once Exo has seen it this session, and stays hidden while Streamer Mode is on. Press a person's name at the top of a DM for their profile.
- **Calls.** Click a voice channel to join it, or the call button at the top of a DM; mute and deafen sit at the right of the band, and a call floats at the foot of the room. Share Your Screen in a call opens Exo's own picker (an app or a screen, with its sound), and Turn On Camera shares your camera.
- **A server.** Its channels are on the left, then the conversation, then its people, always on screen. On a smaller window the people give way first, then the channels move to a dropdown on the channel's name, so neither ever covers the chat. **...** on the title line holds Pinned messages and Notification settings.
- **Pin to top bar.** Right-click a server or a DM (its tile, row, chip, or a server's name over its channels) and choose **Pin to top bar**. It stays beside Home, Messages and Servers with its unread and mention marks, for that Discord account; drag to reorder. When the bar is full the rest are under **...** at the end.
- **Servers.** On the Servers page, drag a server to a new place, or onto another to make a folder; Discord's own apps show the same order. A discord.gg or discord.com/invite link joins the server right here.
- **Play.** Off until you turn on **Show Play** in Settings › Discord › Chat. Then the room has a fourth place with Games (each game with who is on it, and a room per game), Party (who is ready to play one game together, with **Join voice** and **Launch together**, which messages nobody) and Clips (share a capture to a friend or group, asking first). Friends on Exo see that a game is installing, building its shaders or ready only if you turn on **Share when I'm getting ready** in Settings › Discord › Privacy.
- **Menus.** Right-click a person for their menu (with Timeout, Kick, Ban and Roles where you have the permission), a message for its menu, or a picture to copy or save it. **Report** on someone else's message, on a person, on a server you don't own or on an event asks Discord's own questions step by step, then offers Block and Ignore. Right-click a server for everything Discord's menu has there (Server settings straight to a page, Create, Move to, Copy server info), and a server folder for its name and color. Right-click a thread or forum post (in the sidebar, the Threads list or a forum) for Mute, Notification settings, Join or Follow, and for its owner or a moderator Edit tags, Pin, Close and Lock; the threads you're in show under their channel. Right-click an emoji in the picker to add it to your favorites.
- **Settings.** Exo's Settings › Discord holds them, on six pages: Voice and video (noise suppression, echo cancellation, Push to Talk, sensitivity, attenuation, camera), Chat (what messages show, text size, motion, Streamer Mode, Developer Mode), Notifications (with every sound), Privacy, Account (your profile, connected apps and **Log out**) and Keybinds. Settings that belong to your Discord account change it everywhere you use Discord. Exo keeps Discord's data and privacy switches off for you (data to improve and personalize Discord, sponsored content, your messages on web pages, profile-update pings, messages from friends in games), and your account's language and time format follow your PC's, so those have no switches.

Notifications follow your Discord notification settings and go away when you read the chat, here or in any Discord app. Discord's terms don't allow other clients, so use it at your own risk.

## Music

Pick Spotify, Apple Music or YouTube Music in **Settings > Music**, then press **Sign in** in the Music room and sign in on the service's own page. Spotify needs Spotify Premium. **Songs**, under Home, lists every song you saved. A Spotify link someone sends you in Discord opens here, and pasting a Jam link into **Jam** joins it. The mini player in the top bar controls playback from every room.

If the Music room stays empty or signed out, check that the service works in your browser, then sign in again from the Music room.

## Captures

The Capture button in the top bar, or its keys (set in **Capture settings**, in the Capture menu and in **Captures**), saves an instant replay, starts or stops a recording, or takes a snip. **Captures** lists them by game, with trim, favorites, copies sized for Discord or vertical video, **Make reel** (on a game, its latest session's clips, or its starred ones when two or more are starred; or on the clips you pick with **Select**), which joins them in the order they were made into one new clip, and **Send to Discord**, which copies the file and opens the Discord room for you to paste it. While you aren't playing (and a laptop is plugged in), Exo re-saves your recordings at about a third of the size with the audio untouched, one at a time, biggest first, after checking each new file looks the same as the original; the original goes to the Recycle Bin only then. **Capture settings > Make recordings smaller** turns it off, and **Make smaller** on a recording does that one now. A capture holds the game's sound and your microphone, each also on its own track; a Discord call's voices are left out unless you turn on **Capture settings > Record the call's voices**, which also tells everyone in a call in Exo, as Discord's clips do. A red dot beside **Voice connected** means someone else in the call has clips on.

**Speech** in **Capture settings** is off until you ask. **Get** downloads one speech model (66 MB, once; **Remove** takes it away) that runs only on this PC and sends nothing anywhere. With it, **Dictation in Notes** adds a mic to a note's tools: hold it (or Ctrl+Shift+Space) and talk, let go and your words go in at the caret; the microphone is open only while you hold it. **Caption new clips** has Exo find the words in each new replay or recording by itself, and **Captions** on any clip does that one: both wait until no game plays and the PC is quiet, then the words appear over the clip word by word, off until you press **Captions**, and are burned into a copy only when you save one (**For Discord**, **Full quality** or **Vertical**) with Captions on.

**Notes** in Captures (or **New note** and **Open notes** in the Capture menu, or Ctrl+K) holds your notes: each is a plain text or Markdown file in `Documents\Exo Notes` (your user folder's `Exo Notes` when Documents is kept in OneDrive), saved as you type and named for its first line. The notes you had open come back as tabs. **Open** (Ctrl+O) edits any text file where it is; it is written only when you press **Save** (Ctrl+S), and its edits wait on this PC until then, after a restart too. Ctrl+F finds and Ctrl+H replaces, a right-click on an underlined word gives Windows' spelling suggestions, and **Text** or **Markdown** sets a note's kind; a Markdown note has a formatting bar and **Preview**, where a checklist's boxes tick. To open .txt, .md and .log files with Exo, **More > Open text files with Exo** adds Exo to Windows' Open with and Default apps and opens Default apps, where you pick **Exo Notes**; Exo never changes your defaults itself. **More > Stop opening text files with Exo** takes it back.

## Devices

**Devices** shows your mouse, keyboard, what you listen on and your controller, each with its picture; press one to open its page. On your headphones' page, **Pick** the pair you wear (Windows can't tell which pair is on the jack) and set up Lucid, Game Mode and Night Shift. Lucid, by tekkusai, comes with Exo: **Set up** says what it will do first (on most PCs, download Equalizer APO, which asks Windows' permission and for your devices, then a restart). It runs out of sight while Exo does, and your EQ keeps playing when Exo closes, though switching it per game waits for Exo to be back. If you already ran your own Lucid, turning on **Lucid**'s switch moves to the copy in Exo with your settings as they are, and stops yours starting at sign-in; your own file stays where it is, and is used only while it's newer. **Repair** opens Lucid's own window, the one place it shows. Keyboards are marked Coming soon.

For your controller, open its card. **Test** checks its sticks, **Measure** its polling rate. On a DualSense or DualSense Edge connected with a cable, **Calibrate** runs its own center and range routine (leave the sticks alone, then roll both all the way round); **Undo** puts back what it had; an Edge gets Exo's calibration back each time it connects. **Lights and sound** sets its light bar, player light, microphone, speaker and vibration, and **Game profiles** change them for the games you link. Exo sets Steam Input for every Steam game by itself: off where the game has full controller support and reads a PlayStation controller too (**Games read it themselves** says how many), on where it has partial or no controller support, and Steam's own default where it reads only Xbox controllers, so a DualSense still works there. Exo never changes a controller while a game runs.

The Controller Lab also tests a controller's triggers and buttons. The **USB map** button on Devices' title line shows every USB controller and what hangs from it, with each device's link speed and polling rate; it names no sockets, because Windows can't say which is which.

For your mouse, plug in a supported mouse or its receiver and open its card. Only the settings your mouse has are shown; every change is written to the mouse and read back. Save a profile, link it to games, and it switches on by itself when one starts. If the mouse isn't found, wake it, check its battery and connection, then open Devices again. The mouse's CPU core panel tests how steadily its reports arrive, counts a wireless mouse's dropouts, and can record which driver makes reports late (one permission prompt); it names a driver only when its own time explains the gap. Spread them moves your network, audio and spare USB controllers to a quiet core (one permission prompt and a restart, with Undo); it is never part of Best settings and waits until your game is closed.

## Your data

Everything Exo keeps is in `%LocalAppData%\ExoLauncher`, your captures in your Videos folder and your notes in `Documents\Exo Notes`. Exo has no account, ads, analytics or telemetry. The [privacy policy](../PRIVACY.md) lists every network request.

## Report a problem

Check [existing issues](https://github.com/UhhErix/ExoLauncher/issues) first, then [open a report](https://github.com/UhhErix/ExoLauncher/issues/new/choose). Include your Exo version (Settings > General) and, if you can, the log at `%LocalAppData%\ExoLauncher\logs\app.log`. Exo removes passwords, keys and account ids from it, but read it before you post.
