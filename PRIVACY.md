# Exo Launcher privacy statement

**Exo Launcher is local-first. It has no Exo account, no ads, no analytics and no usage tracking. It sends diagnostic reports about crashes, freezes and failures, on by default; see Other network requests.**

Your library, installs, updates, game launches and settings work offline. Exo has no server of its own that they depend on.

## What stays on this PC

Exo keeps its data in `%LocalAppData%\ExoLauncher`. This includes:

- settings (`settings.json`, which also holds your Library folders), the library cache, playtime Exo recorded, hours Xbox reported (`reported-hours.json`: game titles and minutes only), window position, cover art and thumbnail caches, achievement cache and icons (`achievement-art`), and upscaler backups;
- for frame generation you turned on in a game, the games it is in (`frame-gen\games.json`) and Exo's copies of the mods' releases. In the game's own folder Exo writes the mod beside the game's program (MFG Unlocker: one DLL and its settings, `RTXMFG-Universal.json`, with the multiplier you chose; OptiScaler: one DLL, `OptiScaler.ini` and an `OptiScaler` folder) and a record of what it wrote (`exo-frame-gen.json`); the mod writes its own logs and status there as it runs, which Exo reads after a session to say whether frame generation ran. With OptiScaler on an NVIDIA card whose driver caps the frame rate, the game's own NVIDIA profile turns that cap off and OptiScaler's settings take it. Turning it off removes all of it and puts the profile's value back;
- for the FPS counter, if you turn it on: nothing is saved but your choices. While a game runs and is in front, Windows' own event tracing tells Exo when that game presents a frame. Nothing goes in the game, and Exo doesn't open it for this. Anything you add to it (GPU, CPU, memory) is read from Windows while it shows and kept nowhere. If Windows doesn't already let Exo trace, turning it on asks through Windows' own prompt to give your account permission for Exo's one trace session and the three graphics event sources it reads, and if that isn't enough, to add your account to the Performance Log Users group. Those stay after you turn it off. You can remove the group in Computer Management › Local Users and Groups, and the permission is the values named `7e4c6a5e-93b1-4e3c-8f3a-2b1d6c0f5a91`, `ca11c036-0102-4a2d-a6ad-f03cfed5d3c9`, `783aca0a-790e-4d7f-8451-aa850511c6b9` and `802ec45a-1e99-4b83-9920-87c98277ba9d` under `HKLM\SYSTEM\CurrentControlSet\Control\WMI\Security`;
- copies of custom game covers you choose. They are stored locally and never uploaded;
- your Store wishlist (`store/wishlist-v2.json`) and the last sale price Exo told you about, the Store's saved pages (`store/home-v5.json`, Steam's public facts about the games it showed in `store/items-v2.json`, tag names in `store/tags-v1.json`), and your Exo Mouse profiles (`mouse/profiles.json`);
- the parts Exo fetches when it installs: the store tools (Legendary, gogdl, Nile) and their data, and NVIDIA's TitaNet model for Only My Voice (in `tools`, `voice` and `models`; uninstalling Exo removes them), plus the browser data from the Epic and GOG sign-in windows;
- the speech model for dictation and captions (`speech` under Exo's data folder, 66 MB to download, about 70 MB on disk): whisper.cpp's command-line program and libraries and OpenAI's Whisper "base" model in whisper.cpp's quantised form. It is fetched only when you press Get in Captures settings › Speech, never by setup or in the background, and checked against a pinned size and SHA-256 before it is kept or run; Remove, and uninstalling Exo, delete it. The program runs on this PC's processor in its own process, below normal priority, and sends nothing anywhere;
- Exo Shaders, the shader engine behind PC > Graphics > Shaders, keeps its compile records, recordings and settings in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\SCSKiller` (the same folder SCSKiller uses). Exo passes it your installed games' names and folders so it can find their shaders; nothing leaves your PC (see **Exo Shaders** under **Other network requests** for the files it may download). It runs only when it has work, and Exo keeps in `shaders-engine.json` what it saw last time (your graphics driver's version, short hashes of your installed games and their programs, and when it ran) so it starts only when something changed;
- Exo Capture, the add-on behind the Capture button, saves the instant replays, recordings and snips you ask for in your captures folder (`Videos\Exo Captures` unless you choose another), and keeps its settings, thumbnails and which game each capture belongs to in `capture` under Exo's data folder. Captions (off until you turn on Caption new clips, or press Captions on a clip, in the Captures sheet; both need the speech model below) are the words of a clip's microphone track with the time each is said, found on this PC while no game plays and the PC isn't busy, and kept in `capture\captions` by the clip's id; the clip's sound goes to the speech program as a file in `speech\work` that is deleted when it is done. They are drawn over the clip in Exo's own editor and burned into a copy only when you save one with Captions on; a capture you delete takes its captions with it. It captures the game's window or the screen through Windows; only when Windows gets no picture from a game (exclusive full screen) does it load OBS Studio's game capture hook into that game to copy its frames, never into Riot games or while FACEIT or ESEA runs. Every replay and recording has the mix of the game (with no game, the apps playing on this PC) and your default microphone, and each of the two on a track of its own. A Discord call's voices, in Exo or in the Discord app, are left out: on no track and not in the mix. With no game, so are apps that play a call or a microphone again (Discord, mixers such as Voicemeeter, Sonar or Wave Link, voice changers, Teams and Zoom). Capture settings › Record the call's voices (off unless you turn it on) puts the call in, on a track of its own and in the mix; then, while instant replay or a recording runs during a call in Exo's Discord room, Exo flags you in the call as having clips on, as Discord's own clips do, so everyone in it is told. When someone else in your call has clips on, Exo's voice bar shows a red dot. Mute a track in your player or editor to leave it out of a file. While instant replay or a recording runs, your microphone is in use and Windows shows its microphone indicator. Instant replay holds the last minutes in memory, by default only while a game is running, and writes nothing until you save. Captures never leave your PC by themselves: Send to Discord copies the file and opens the Discord room for you to paste it into a chat. A storage limit or age you set sends the oldest captures to the Recycle Bin; favorites are always kept. A capture you delete goes to the Recycle Bin too;
- Exo Notes (Captures › Notes) keeps your notes as plain text and Markdown files in `Documents\Exo Notes` (your user folder's `Exo Notes` when Documents is kept in OneDrive), and in `notes` under Exo's data folder which notes are open, their pins and the game each was written in, and the edits of a file opened from elsewhere until you save it. A word you right-click is checked by Windows' own spell checker on this PC; Add to dictionary puts it in Windows' word list. Nothing in a note is sent anywhere. Dictation (off until you turn it on in Captures settings › Speech, and it needs the speech model below) opens your microphone only while you hold the mic or Ctrl+Shift+Space in a note, never otherwise, and not while a game plays or the PC is busy; what you say is kept as sound in memory for that one take, handed to the speech program on this PC as a file in `speech\work` that is deleted the moment it is done (and swept at start), and only the words come back into the note. Exo adds itself to Windows' Open with and Default apps for .txt, .md and .log files (for your account only) only when you choose Open text files with Exo; Windows keeps which app opens them, and Stop opening text files with Exo, or uninstalling Exo, takes it out.
- the browser profile used for YouTube Music and Apple Music;
- logs (see **Logs** below).

PC > Apps keeps on this PC what it needs to clear up after a program and to put that back: every program it has seen with its folder and when each last ran (`apps-seen.json`, read from Windows' own records of what ran), and what it removed (`removals.json`, and the registry entries it saved first in `cleanup`). Nothing of it leaves your PC.

To find your games, Exo reads local folders, store manifests, registry keys and data saved by store clients on this PC. For hours, it also reads GOG Galaxy 2.0's database (`galaxy-2.0.db`, read-only) for the time Galaxy holds for games on the other platforms you connected to it. Exo does not upload your library, install folders, launch settings, playtime or achievements. Some services see a game title or store id when Exo looks up artwork or hours for it; they are listed under **Other network requests**.

To free memory, Exo reads the process list on this PC (names, parent processes, and the command line of a Battle.net process to tell its renderers apart) to find the browser helpers of Steam, the Epic Games Launcher, the EA app, Ubisoft Connect, GOG Galaxy and Battle.net, and lowers their memory use while they are hidden. It reads nothing inside them, keeps nothing and sends nothing.

**Keys and sign-ins stay local.** Optional keys you paste (Steam Web API, itch.io, SteamGridDB, OpenXBL) and your saved Riot ID are encrypted with Windows (DPAPI) for your Windows user. They are not written to `settings.json`, not shown back in the app, and only sent to the service they belong to (see **Other network requests**). Other programs running as the same Windows user can still get at them.

Store sign-ins stay with the official tools:

- Epic sign-in goes to Legendary.
- GOG sign-in goes to gogdl.
- Amazon sign-in goes to Nile.

Exo opens the store's own sign-in page, passes the result to that tool, and does not keep its own copy of the token.

**Accounts in Settings > Library.** To show who each store is signed in as, Exo reads the display names in Steam's `config\loginusers.vdf`, Legendary's `user.json`, Nile's `user.json`, and the Epic Games Launcher's account id in the registry (to tell whether it uses a different account). Only display names reach the page; account names, ids and tokens are never logged. Switch account on Steam does what Steam's own account picker does: it closes Steam, marks that account as the one to sign in to in `loginusers.vdf` and the registry (`AutoLoginUser`, `RememberPassword`), and starts Steam again. Exo never sees a Steam password and never touches `config.vdf`. Epic accounts you add for their hours each get their own Legendary folder in `%LocalAppData%\ExoLauncher\epic-accounts`, and the last Epic playtime of each account signed in on this PC is kept in `epic-playtime.json` under a one-way tag. Exo adds up the hours each Steam account on this PC and each of those Epic accounts recorded; nothing is sent anywhere new.

**Store settings Exo changes.** For each installed Steam game Exo turns the Steam overlay on where the game needs it (purchases through Steam, playing online with friends, Remote Play Together, Steam Input) and off elsewhere, sets Steam Input from the game's controller support, and keeps Steam's and Epic's own pop-ups and sounds off. Exo writes these switches into each Steam account's `localconfig.vdf` and `sharedconfig.vdf` and into Epic's own settings, on this PC, while those apps are closed; nothing else in those files changes, and a switch you change in Steam afterwards stays yours. While Steam is open, Exo also turns a game's cloud saves back on, sets a game that updates only when launched back to Steam's automatic updates and one that always downloads while it runs back to Steam's own setting, through Steam's own calls, as its Properties page does. When you press Play on a Steam game, Exo accepts that game's own EULA and Steam's default launch choice if Steam asks, never Steam's Subscriber Agreement or a purchase.

**When Steam's servers are down.** While Steam runs, Exo reads two of Steam's own logs on this PC every 15 seconds (`logs\connection_log.txt` and `logs\steamui_login.txt`: whether Steam's sign-in was answered, and Steam's own internet check) to tell you once when Steam can't reach its servers; nothing is sent anywhere and nothing from them is kept. If you choose Play offline, Exo closes Steam (never while a Steam game runs), turns on Steam's own offline mode for the account Steam signs in as (`WantsOfflineMode` and `SkipOfflineModeWarning` in `config\loginusers.vdf`, what Steam's Go Offline writes), and starts Steam again. It first keeps a copy of that file (`steam-offline-loginusers.vdf`) and the two values it had (`steam-offline.json`) in Exo's data folder, puts exactly those values back when Steam's servers answer again (or after you go online in Steam), and then deletes both. Nothing else in the file changes, and no password or token is read.

**Gifts.** To show gifts waiting for you in the Library, Exo opens Steam's and Epic's own gifts pages in a hidden browser with its own profile (`gifts-webview` in Exo's data folder) when you open the Library (at most every half hour) or refresh it, and every half hour while Exo is in front and you're not playing, and reads them; Accept and Decline make the same calls those pages make. Steam's website needs one sign-in there, on Steam's own page (Settings > Game stores > Steam gifts); it stays in that profile until you sign out there. Epic's store page is signed in with a one-time code from Exo's Epic sign-in. Gift names, senders and messages are shown, never saved, logged or sent anywhere else.

## Exo accounts (removed)

Exo 3.4 removed the optional Exo account. The app has no sign-in, sign-up, sign-out, handle, badges, export or delete screens any more, and **it never contacts the Exo account service (exo-id)**. Recent versions synced nothing to the account, so your library, playtime, settings and keys are already on this PC and keep working as before.

**On this PC.** The first time Exo 3.4 or later starts, it deletes what an earlier version saved for the account, in `%LocalAppData%\ExoLauncher` and `%AppData%\ExoLauncher`:

- `auth.bin`, the session token, encrypted with Windows (DPAPI), that kept you signed in, together with the account id, handle and email it held, plus any unfinished copy (`auth.bin.tmp`);
- the `online-cache` folder of saved account responses;
- `device-id`, the per-PC id very old versions sent with a library activity summary.

This is a local cleanup only: Exo does not sign out with the service or send it anything. It runs again on every start, so a copy restored from a backup is removed too. Exo writes one line to its log when it removes a saved sign-in.

**On the service.** If you created an account with an earlier version, the service still holds what it held before: your account id, display name, email, a salted password hash, session records (which may include an IP address and an app or browser identifier), and anything older versions stored, such as a handle, profile fields, a library activity summary, online status, preferences, friends or store links. Exo 3.4 does not add to it or change it. That data stays on the service (Cloudflare Workers with a Cloudflare D1 database) until the service is shut down and its data deleted. Until then, Exo 3.3.x can still download a copy (Settings > Exo account > Download) or delete the account (Settings > Exo account > Delete account); deleting removes the account and everything linked to it. Sessions end after 7 days, and the service removes expired ones every hour.

## Connected apps

Exo's Discord room (its own client) and Music room talk to those services for you, with your own sign-in. Each service keeps its own data and privacy policy. What Exo reads from them (your messages, your library, what you played) is shown to you and not stored, logged or sent anywhere else.

### Discord

- **Exo's own Discord client.** The Discord room is Exo's own client; Exo does not run, install or change the Discord desktop app. You sign in by scanning a QR code with Discord's phone app, so Exo never sees your password. Exo keeps the sign-in on this PC, encrypted for your Windows account (`discord-client.bin` in Exo's data folder), and sends it only to Discord. Messages are shown, never saved or logged, and only channels your roles let you see are shown. What you were writing and haven't sent (up to 100 conversations) is kept on this PC in Exo's page storage, as Discord's app keeps drafts, and goes when you send or clear it. Exo sends Discord your status and your custom status, never the song Exo's Music room plays and never the game you're playing. With them goes one thing only other Exo apps use: your friend link's public key, so friends who use Exo can find you (see **Exo's relay**). It is off until you turn on **Share my games with friends on Exo** in Settings › Discord › Privacy, for each Discord account. Exo leaves your account's own Share my activity setting as it is, on or off. In a call, your song goes only to the other Exo apps in that call, end-to-end encrypted, through Exo's relay (see **Exo's relay**). Each time it connects, Exo also turns off your Discord account's data and privacy switches wherever one is on: Data & Privacy's two consents (data to improve Discord, data to personalize it), sponsored content from your activity and from third-party data, your messages on Discord's web pages, telling friends when you update your profile, and messages from friends in connected games. It sets your account's language and time format to your PC's. These are your Discord account's own settings, saved with Discord as its app saves them, so they hold on your other devices too. Listen along reads a friend's song from the activity Discord already sends Exo, or, for someone in your call who uses Exo, from Exo's relay, and looks for that song's name and artist on your own music service, through the service's own app or page; nothing about it goes anywhere else. An invite link you click is sent to Discord to join that server. A capture you share from Play › Clips is read from your captures folder, up to 25 MB, and sent to Discord as an attachment in the conversation you pick, only after you press Share, choose the conversation and press Send; it is kept in memory for that and nowhere else. In a call, your microphone is used only while you're in it, in Discord settings > Voice & Video only while its level meter shows, and for a voice message only from when you press its button until you stop or delete it, at most 20 minutes (Windows shows its microphone indicator); a voice message stays in memory until you send it to Discord or delete it, and is never saved or logged; its sound is cleaned of noise and echo on this PC (DeepFilterNet and WebRTC's echo canceller, or NVIDIA's noise removal on an RTX card once you download it); with Only My Voice on, Exo learns your voice by itself as you talk (your first 15 seconds in a call or on the Voice & Video meter, then speech that is clearly yours), keeps that voiceprint (192 numbers) on this PC, encrypted for your Windows account (`voice\my-voice.bin`), and compares voices with it only on this PC; Start over or Forget deletes it; encrypted end to end with Discord's DAVE protocol and sent only to Discord's voice server. Mentions and DMs show as Windows notifications with the message's text, as Discord's app does. Discord settings change your Discord account's own settings on Discord, as Discord's apps do; what Discord keeps per device (Accessibility, Streamer Mode, the camera, keybinds, sounds) stays on this PC. Your email and phone number are shown masked and never reach the page in full. Discord's sounds and the fonts of its display name styles are fetched once from discord.com and kept in Exo's data folder. The Shop shows Discord's catalog and the collectibles you own. Buying with a card already saved on your Discord account happens through Discord, which shows Exo only the card's brand, last four digits and expiry; Exo never sees or stores card numbers or your billing address, and anything else finishes in Discord's own shop in your browser. Keybinds read the keys you hold only while you're in a call, and never record or send them. Sign out removes the sign-in and ends it on Discord. A link in a message, a note or the picture viewer is checked for a look-alike of a well-known brand's address (disc0rd, discord-nitro-gift) by its spelling alone, on this PC, with nothing looked up or sent; one that imitates a brand asks before it opens. Discord's terms do not allow third-party clients; that risk comes with signing in.

### Spotify

Spotify plays in its own web player (open.spotify.com), hidden behind Exo on Exo's own web profile, where you sign in on Spotify's own page. Exo doesn't start, change, install or close the Spotify desktop app. If you run it yourself, Exo only reads the song it plays from Windows' media controls while you are in a Discord call, for the song your call's Exo apps see.

**Recording** is off until you turn it on, on the Shaders page: it puts a small `d3d12.dll` in each game's folder that records which pipelines the game creates, stays on this PC, and refuses games with anti-cheat.

**Prepare** on the Shaders page starts an Unreal game when you press it, or by itself while nobody is using the PC (a switch on that page turns that off), watches the size of your graphics driver's shader cache and the game's processor use, and closes the game when both go quiet. It stays on this PC, and it keeps only which driver each game was prepared for.

**Exo's Music room for Spotify** draws Spotify's pages itself. Through the web player's page, it asks Spotify for what the room shows (your library, home, search, playlists, albums, artists, podcasts, the queue, devices, recently played, lyrics of the playing song, your friends' activity, and your Jam) and passes on what you click (play, queue, like, add to or remove from a playlist, start, leave or end a Jam, remove someone from it). Spotify makes every one of these requests itself, with its own sign-in, exactly as when you click in Spotify; Exo never reads your token or cookies. Only small summaries (names, picture links, durations) reach Exo's window, and nothing is stored. A Jam's invite link reaches Exo only to be copied when you press Copy invite link. Album art, profile pictures and lyrics' covers load in Exo's window straight from Spotify's image servers (`*.scdn.co`, `*.spotifycdn.com`).

### Your mouse (Exo Mouse)

In the Devices room, your mouse's page is Exo Mouse, which is part of Exo's download and talks to your mouse directly over USB HID. It reads and writes your mouse's own settings (such as DPI, polling rate, buttons, lighting, battery level and onboard profiles). That data stays on this PC: profiles you save in Exo Mouse (a name, the settings to put back and the games they are for) are kept in Exo's data folder on this PC (`mouse\profiles.json`), nothing is sent anywhere, and Exo needs no OpenMouse app, vendor software or driver for it. To show a picture of your mouse, Exo Mouse loads its product render from OpenMouse's image server (img.openmouse.app), the request names the model's image file, and the image is then kept in Exo's browser cache. Your mouse's card in the Devices room shows the picture Exo's list has for that same render (the render itself, or the maker's own picture where it is sharper). Your keyboard, controller and headphones are shown with their maker's own product picture (where the maker publishes none large or clean enough, an authorized store's copy of it). Exo picks it from a list of links built into Exo, by what Windows and the device report (its name, a keyboard's layout, a DualSense's body color) or the headphones you picked in Lucid, and requests only that image file from the maker's image server (see **Device pictures** below). Nothing about you or this PC goes with the request, and no cookies; as when you open the maker's product page, the image server sees which product's picture was asked for. Exo fits the picture to the Devices room's frame and keeps it on this PC (`device-images` in Exo's data folder), so each picture is fetched once. A device with no picture in Exo's list is drawn by Exo itself.

Earlier versions of Exo answered the OpenMouse web panel through a bridge of their own and could stop the OpenMouse Bridge app to do it. That bridge is gone. The first time this version starts, if Exo had stopped the OpenMouse Bridge app, Exo puts its start with Windows back exactly as it was and writes the profiles Exo kept into the app's own settings file, then deletes Exo's copy. It does not start the app.

### Lucid

Lucid, the headphone EQ on your audio's page, is a separate program by tekkusai that comes inside Exo's download and runs only once you set it up. Its settings stay in its own folders on this PC (`%APPDATA%\LucidEQ`, `%ProgramData%\LucidEQ`), and Exo reads and writes them there. Exo itself makes no request for Lucid and never downloads a Lucid other than the one that came with it. Two requests are Lucid's own, not Exo's, and go to its maker's GitHub repository (raw.githubusercontent.com/tekkusai/Lucid): when you press Set up on a PC without Equalizer APO, that installer; and when Lucid starts, its newest version number, which says nothing about you. It offers that update only in its own window, and downloads it only if you press Update there. "Show Lucid in Discord", off until you turn it on, shows it in your Discord status through the Discord app on this PC.

### YouTube Music

YouTube Music runs inside Exo as a web page (Microsoft Edge WebView2), using a browser profile stored under `%LocalAppData%\ExoLauncher`. You sign in to Google there, and Google handles that data.

Exo's Music room draws YouTube Music's pages itself and keeps that page out of sight behind it (it shows only so you can sign in). Exo asks the page for what the room shows (home, search, your library, playlists, albums, artists, the queue, lyrics) and passes on what you click (play, like, shuffle, repeat); the page makes every request itself with your Google sign-in, as when you click in it, and Exo never reads its cookies or tokens. Only small summaries reach Exo's window, and nothing is stored. Covers load in Exo's window straight from Google's image servers (`*.googleusercontent.com`, `i.ytimg.com`). YouTube Music's lyrics on the web aren't timed, so when you open Lyrics, Exo looks the song up in LRCLIB's open lyrics library for timed ones (see **Synced lyrics for YouTube Music** below).

### Apple Music

Apple Music runs inside Exo as Apple's own web player (music.apple.com) in the same browser profile. You sign in to Apple there, in Apple's own sign-in window, and Apple handles that data. Exo's Music room draws Apple Music's pages itself and keeps that page out of sight behind it (it shows only so you can sign in). Exo asks the page's own Apple Music player (MusicKit) for what the room shows (home, recently played, search, your library, playlists, albums, artists, the queue, lyrics of the playing song) and passes on what you click (play, add to the queue, favorite, add to or remove from your library or a playlist, shuffle, repeat, volume). The page makes every request itself, with Apple's own developer token and your sign-in; Exo never reads, stores or copies those tokens or its cookies, with one step inside Apple's page: as Apple's sign-in hands the page your music token, a small script there also saves it in that page's own cookie, where Apple's player looks for it, so the sign-in holds. It never reaches Exo's window. Music plays inside Apple's page, protected as Apple protects it; Exo never handles the audio. Only small summaries (names, picture links, durations) reach Exo's window, and nothing is stored. Covers load in Exo's window straight from Apple's image servers (`*.mzstatic.com`). The Apple Music app for Windows is no longer used.

Exo adds these browser extensions to that profile:

- **Exo Shield**, Exo's own blocker, blocks ads, trackers and cookie notices with the lists built into it (uBlock Origin Lite's and HaGeZi's Multi PRO), and downloads no lists of its own.
- **SponsorBlock** asks sponsor.ajay.app about the video that is playing.

These extensions have their own privacy policies. A music page that asks for your microphone, camera, clipboard or notifications asks you first; Settings > Music sets YouTube Music's notifications.

## Exo's relay

Exo's one server piece. It passes sealed messages between Exo apps for two things, so that Discord is told neither: your song inside a Discord call, and what you're playing, for friends who use Exo. Discord's own **Share my activity** has nothing to do with it. **Who runs it:** Exo Launcher's developer, on Cloudflare's Workers platform, which carries the connection.

### In a call

In a Discord call that is end-to-end encrypted (Discord's DAVE), the Exo apps in the call show each other what their Music room plays, so you can listen along with someone in your call.

- **What is sent:** your song in Exo's Music room (its title, artists, length, where it is, whether it plays, the service and that service's id for it), or that nothing plays; and your Discord user id, so the others know it's yours. All of it is sealed with a key made from the call's own end-to-end encryption, which only the people in that call have. Discord never gets it, and the relay can't read it.
- **What the relay sees:** your PC's IP address, a random name for the call that changes whenever someone joins or leaves, how many Exo apps share it, and when and how big each sealed message is (rounded up to one of four sizes). It never sees who you are, which call, or what plays.
- **What it keeps:** each connection's last sealed message, in memory, to hand to whoever joins the call after you; it goes when you leave. Nothing is written to disk, and nothing is logged.
- **When:** only while you're in an end-to-end encrypted call in Exo's Discord room. Leaving clears your song for everyone in the call. In a call that isn't end-to-end encrypted, nothing is sent.

### Between friends

Friends who use Exo see the game you opened from Exo on their Home, and Discord and its apps don't. This is **off until you turn it on**, for each Discord account: **Share my games with friends on Exo** in Settings › Discord › Privacy. While it is off, no key is put in your presence, no connection is made, and nothing is sent or heard; turning it off again takes your key out of your presence and ends every connection.

- **What is sent:** while a game you opened from Exo runs, its title and the time you started it; otherwise, that nothing runs. Never a game Exo didn't open, one you hid from your Library or one you added yourself, and nothing at all while you're Invisible. It goes only to actual Discord friends who show a key and are online (not server members, friend requests or people you ignored), to 16 of them at most at a time, the ones you last messaged first. It is sealed with a key only you and that friend can work out. Discord never gets it, and the relay can't read it.
- **What Discord gets:** your friend link's public key, 44 characters, as a field of your custom status activity in your presence (on your real custom status if you have one, which stays as it is). Discord's apps don't show it, and no game or Discord sign-in goes with it. Anyone who can read your raw presence through their own software could see the key, and tell from it that you use Exo with this on.
- **What the relay sees:** your PC's IP address, a random name for each pair of friends, which stays the same from one day to the next, when each side is connected, and when each sealed message is sent; every message is the same size. So the relay and Cloudflare could tell that the same two IP addresses keep sharing a name. They never see who either of you is on Discord, or what you play.
- **What it keeps:** each connection's last sealed message, in memory, for the friend who connects after you; it goes when you disconnect. Nothing is written to disk, and nothing is logged.
- **When:** only while Exo is open and connected to Discord, and a friend with a key is online. With no friend on Exo online, no connection is made.
- **On this PC:** your friend link's private key, encrypted for your Windows account (`friend-link-<your Discord id>.bin` in Exo's data folder); it never leaves this PC, and Sign out deletes it.

**Share when I'm getting ready** in Settings › Discord › Privacy is **off until you turn it on**, and reads off while Share my games is, since it is the same link. On, friends who use Exo see on their Party that a game is installing (how far, in steps of ten), that Exo is building its shaders, or that it is ready (said for 30 minutes, or until you start it). Only a title Share my games may name is ever said, never one you hid or added yourself, to the same friends, sealed the same way and kept the same way (in memory, for the friend who connects after you). It is told only by Exo's own install and shader events; nothing looks at a game's process, window or files. Discord never gets it.

## Logs

Exo writes a diagnostic log to `%LocalAppData%\ExoLauncher\logs\app.log`. It holds up to about 2 MB, plus one older file. The log can include:

- game titles and folder names;
- app and store tool messages;
- errors from the app's interface;
- crash details.

Before writing, Exo removes things that look like tokens, passwords, keys, account or store ids, and email addresses. **The log stays on your PC.** Exo does not upload it, except the scrubbed excerpt (at most 8 KB) that a diagnostic report carries, described in **Other network requests**. The previous run's log is kept as `app.log.1`. **Open logs**, offered when part of Exo stops, only opens the folder. If you share a log for support, check it first.

## What Exo Launcher does not do

- No ads, no analytics and no usage tracking. Exo sends no usage data. Crashes are written to the local log; diagnostic reports (crashes, freezes and failures, on by default) leave your PC as described in **Other network requests**, scrubbed first.
- No Exo account, and no online dependency for your library or game actions.
- No library activity or playtime sent anywhere. The one lasting id is your friend link's public key, one per Discord account on a PC, which goes in your Discord presence. Your online status goes only to Discord (see **Discord**); the game you opened from Exo, and, only if you turn on Share when I'm getting ready, that a game is installing, building its shaders or ready, go only to friends who use Exo, sealed (see **Exo's relay**).
- No uploading of your library, install folders, playtime history or store sign-ins.
- No Exo friends list, profiles, handles, badges, chat or settings sync. The friends, profiles and chat in the Discord room are Discord's own.
- No kernel driver, anti-cheat bypass, or silent approval of Windows security prompts.
- No server anyone else can reach. The OBS browser source for achievement banners (`http://127.0.0.1:9364/achievements`) listens only on this PC's loopback address and answers only requests addressed to it; it serves the banner page and the achievements you unlock while Exo runs, nothing else.
- The clipboard is read only when you open **Activate key**, to spot a game key, and only on this PC. Exo then opens the store's own redeem page (for Epic it copies the key for you to paste); it does not send the key anywhere itself. Exo Capture writes to the clipboard when you snip or press Copy or Send to Discord, and never reads it. In Exo's own Discord client, Copy Image puts the picture on the clipboard and Save Image saves it in your Downloads folder.
